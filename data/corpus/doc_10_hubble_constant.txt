The Hubble constant (H0) measures the rate at which the universe is expanding. Measurements of H0 using the cosmic microwave background (Planck satellite) yield a value of approximately 67.4 km/s/Mpc. However, measurements using local Cepheid variables and supernovae (Hubble Space Telescope) yield a higher value of approximately 73.0 km/s/Mpc. This persistent discrepancy, known as the Hubble Tension, suggests potential gaps in our standard model of cosmology.
