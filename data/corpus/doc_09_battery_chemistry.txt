Lithium-ion batteries are the dominant technology for portable electronics and electric vehicles. They rely on the movement of lithium ions between a cathode (usually made of a transition metal oxide) and an anode (typically graphite) through an electrolyte. The theoretical energy density limit of conventional lithium-ion batteries is around 350 Wh/kg. Solid-state batteries, which replace the liquid electrolyte with a solid material, promise higher energy densities and improved safety.
