The CAP theorem, also known as Brewer's theorem, states that a distributed data store can simultaneously provide at most two of three guarantees: Consistency, Availability, and Partition Tolerance. Consistency means every read receives the most recent write or an error. Availability means every non-failing node returns a non-error response, without guarantee of it being the latest. Partition Tolerance means the system continues to operate despite arbitrary message loss or delay. In practice, networks are never 100% reliable, so systems must choose between Consistency and Availability when a partition occurs.
